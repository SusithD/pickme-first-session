THE PROBLEM

Trust is won or lost in the first session

Public reviews of PickMe in Sri Lanka raise four themes, each reported by roughly five reviewers.

01

Fare above the estimate

Reviewers say the final charge came in well over the fare shown at booking.

02

A trip only the driver can end

Reviewers say the trip stays open after arrival and the meter or card charge keeps going.

03

Cash demanded on card trips

Reviewers say drivers ask for cash although the ride was set up to pay by card.

04

Support that does not answer

Reviewers say tickets are closed without an answer and promised callbacks never came.

Evidence note. About 30 public reviews and posts; several were seen only as search snippets. These are reviewer reports, not verified in the app.

RESEARCH & EVIDENCE

What the desk research can and cannot say

Sources

  • About 30 dated public reviews and posts (Trustpilot, App Store, LinkedIn, press)
  • 6-app benchmark from help pages and articles: Uber, Grab, Gojek, Careem, Bolt, Rapido
  • No interviews. No PickMe data.

Found

  • Four repeated trust themes at fare, trip end, payment and support
  • Benchmark patterns for upfront fares and fare-before-signup

Not found

  • No evidence either way on signup, OTP or permissions. That is a gap, not a finding.
  • No public funnel rates, so none are claimed.

TWO BETS

Fix the fare trust first, then the front door

PART B · PRIMARY

A fare you can hold

If the booking screen shows a fixed fare, its breakdown and the only three things that can change it, and the receipt explains any difference, then riders book and ride again, because reviewers repeatedly report a gap between the fare shown and the fare charged.

Evidence: repeated review reports plus benchmark

PART A · SUPPORTING

Fare before account

If a new rider sees their own fare before signing up and the code step moves to Book, then more reach a first booking, because leading apps show the price first.

Evidence: benchmark only, lower confidence

BEFORE / AFTER

One screen, one promise

01

Fixed fare, not an estimate

The fare is shown as a fixed number rather than a range.

02

Breakdown and change rules

Base, distance and peak are itemised, with the three proposed reasons it could change.

03

Receipt explains the delta

Any difference is listed line by line before payment.

TESTING PLAN & NEXT STEPS

How it would be tested

Primary metric

Intent-to-treat: share of all assigned riders who complete a second ride within 14 days of their first fare view.

Guardrails

Driver acceptance, driver earnings per hour, cancellations, unresolved support.

Planning case

15% baseline and a 3 point lift needs about 2,403 riders per arm. Both numbers are assumptions.

Driver economics

A fixed fare shifts who absorbs variance. The plan states who pays for each change, and it is to be agreed before any test.
