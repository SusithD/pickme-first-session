# First-use tests: guide

Five short sessions (20 to 30 minutes each) with people in Sri Lanka.

## Who
- 3 people who have never used PickMe (for example older relatives, students, or people who only use Uber or call tuk-tuks).
- 2 people who tried PickMe but rarely or never use it now.
- Mix of ages, at least one Sinhala-first or Tamil-first speaker, and at least one person with a low-end Android phone.

## Consent
Explain that this is a personal design project, not for PickMe. Ask before recording the screen or voice. Use only first names or initials in notes. Don't record phone numbers or OTPs.

## Part 1: background (5 minutes)
1. How did you last get a tuk or taxi? Walk me through it.
2. What apps do you use for rides, food or deliveries? Why those?
3. (For lapsed users) What happened the last time you used PickMe? Why did you stop?

## Part 2: first-use task (15 minutes)
Hand over the phone with PickMe freshly installed. Say: "Imagine you need a tuk from here to [a real nearby place]. Get to the point where you would confirm it, then stop."

- Don't help. If they're stuck for over 60 seconds, ask "What are you looking for?"
- Note every hesitation, wrong tap, question and moment of doubt, with the step it happened on.
- Ask them to think aloud.

## Part 3: debrief (5 to 10 minutes)
1. What was the hardest part?
2. Did you trust the fare? Why or why not?
3. How would you pay? Did the app make that clear?
4. Was there anything you expected to see and didn't?
5. On a scale of 1 to 5, how likely are you to use it next time you need a ride? Why?
